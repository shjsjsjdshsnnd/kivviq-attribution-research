## Simulation architecture

Step 4 implements a deterministic-under-seed, event-driven ecommerce simulator.

The simulation kernel contains:

- an explicit monotonic simulation clock;
- a timestamp/priority/sequence ordered discrete-event queue;
- semantic counter-based random draws;
- deterministic random namespaces;
- shared-randomness factual/counterfactual replay.

A stochastic draw is a pure function of:

```
simulation seed
+ random namespace
+ semantic draw key
```

This prevents unrelated randomness from being scrambled merely because an intervention removes one event path.

## Latent customer runtime state

Frozen Step 3 customers are copied into mutable runtime-only God-mode state.

Runtime state includes:

- need;
- intent;
- awareness;
- consideration;
- brand affinity;
- lifecycle;
- purchase count;
- need/replenishment timing;
- per-channel causal memory;
- carryover/decay;
- persistent cart state.

Frozen Step 3 customer definitions are never modified in place.

Marketing carryover has explicit half-life/decay behavior.

Customer intent and need evolve through simulation time rather than receiving timestamps after independent event generation.

## Natural channel selection vs exposure vs causal effect

Step 4 preserves three separate concepts:

1. natural tendency to use a channel;
2. whether a marketing exposure occurs;
3. whether that exposure causally changes latent behavior.

Natural visits/searches are generated from Step 3 natural propensities and latent state.

Paid exposure opportunity is generated separately from merchant channel activity/spend, customer state and randomness.

For an actual exposure, causal response is based on:

```
frozen merchant causal effect
× frozen customer susceptibility multiplier
```

Therefore a merchant channel with true effect zero can still:

- receive impressions/touches;
- be naturally selected by high-intent customers;
- appear in converting paths;
- receive last-touch observational attribution;

while producing exactly zero causal response.

## Observable vs God-mode truth

Perfectly observable journey events are kept separate from causal truth.

Observable behavior can contain:

- impressions;
- email/SMS opens;
- searches;
- visits;
- sessions;
- landing/page views;
- product views;
- add-to-cart;
- checkout;
- purchase;
- abandonment.

God-mode truth separately records:

- exposure-level causal response;
- delay/carryover;
- zero-effect exposures;
- causal channels associated with purchases;
- latent final customer states;
- controlled counterfactual differences.

The Operator-safe root package does not export simulation, counterfactual replay or causal-evaluator functions.

## Commerce and economics

Product selection uses:

- frozen merchant product demand/popularity;
- Step 3 sparse product/category preferences;
- customer current need/intent;
- price response;
- promotion response;
- inventory;
- causal product-preference memory;
- stochastic exploration.

Inventory constrains realized commerce.

Supported frozen Step 1 interventions currently executed by Step 4:

- channel spend `set`;
- product-price `set`;
- promotion active/inactive `set`;
- available inventory `set`.

Interventions change causal inputs/state. They do not directly edit final orders or revenue.

An inventory intervention setting available units to zero produces zero realized orders/revenue through the normal commerce mechanism while the factual world continues to purchase.

### Frozen price-schema limitation

The frozen v1 GroundTruth schema has no authoritative per-product base-price field.

Step 4 therefore derives a deterministic simulation price from frozen Step 2 merchant catalog/AOV diagnostics and product rank.

It does not add arbitrary metadata or modify Steps 1–3.

A future governed GroundTruth schema evolution would be required before exact generated product prices could become authoritative frozen causal state.

## Time, seasonality and shocks

Month/weekday seasonality affects need formation before customer actions occur.

External shocks are applied only during their declared time window and only when their affected variables include relevant demand/order/funnel descendants.

Future shocks do not modify historical events.

## Cross-channel effects

Step 2 channel interactions are applied to latent customer memory when relevant channel histories coexist.

Supported interaction semantics include:

- mediation/carryover;
- synergy;
- cannibalization;
- delayed effects;
- zero interactions.

The zero-paid-effect acceptance fixture explicitly zeros interaction effects so no interaction can manufacture hidden paid incrementality in that test world.

## Shared-randomness counterfactual replay

`replayCounterfactual` simulates:

```
factual:
same merchant
same latent population
same simulation seed
no intervention

counterfactual:
same merchant
same latent population
same simulation seed
specified intervention
```

Semantic keyed random draws preserve controlled stochastic structure across the two worlds.

The replay reports:

- represented order delta;
- represented revenue delta;
- represented contribution-profit delta;
- individual customer purchase/revenue changes.

## Platform-style observational metrics

The simulator also produces intentionally observational channel metrics.

Current research metric behavior includes last-touch-style attributed revenue and purchases.

Those metrics are computed only from observable paths.

They do not read the God-mode causal ledger.

This makes it possible for observational channel performance and causal truth to disagree.

## Hard zero-paid-effect acceptance

A dedicated adversarial world sets every modeled paid channel's merchant-level true causal effect to exactly zero while preserving natural channel selection and exposure opportunity.

The test additionally zeros paid cross-channel interactions.

The simulator still produces:

- paid-channel touches;
- realistic paid-touched converting paths;
- positive platform-style attributed paid revenue;
- channels that appear observationally effective;
- direct/organic commerce;
- non-converting journeys.

Shared-randomness paid-media-off replay verifies:

```
true incremental paid revenue = 0
true incremental paid orders  = 0
```

even though observational metrics can attribute positive revenue to paid channels.

This is the central Step 4 correlation ≠ attribution ≠ causation acceptance condition.

## Selection-bias demonstrations

The validation harness verifies that at least one zero-effect touched cohort can look better observationally than its untouched cohort on:

- purchase rate;
- revenue per represented customer; or
- repeat-buyer rate.

This arises from Step 3 natural selection and latent customer heterogeneity, not from hard-coded attribution bias.

Promotion acceptance separately requires both discounted and full-price buyers and verifies that realized discounted buyers have higher average promotion sensitivity.

## Identical observable journey, different causal truth

The simulator/evaluator demonstrates the shortcut-risk requirement directly.

It first finds one realized purchase whose observable path includes both Meta and Google Search and whose hidden exposure ledger contains actual Meta and Google marketing exposures in the purchase lookback window.

The observable purchase/event stream is then held fixed.

The God-mode evaluator applies four hidden merchant-effect specifications to that exact same realized path:

### A — Meta caused / Google zero

```
Meta: nonzero
Google Search: zero
```

Causal channels: Meta only.

### B — Google caused / Meta zero

```
Meta: zero
Google Search: nonzero
```

Causal channels: Google Search only.

### C — both contributed

```
Meta: nonzero
Google Search: nonzero
```

Causal channels: Meta + Google Search.

### D — neither caused

```
Meta: zero
Google Search: zero
```

Causal channels: none.

The observed journey is identical in all four evaluations.

This proves that an attribution learner cannot safely infer causal truth from path shape alone.

## Nonzero causal recovery

A separate controlled world re-enables substantial nonzero Meta, Google Search and Pinterest causal effects.

Shared-randomness paid-media-off replay across controlled seeds produces positive aggregate incremental paid revenue.

The validation suite therefore checks both sides:

- true zero effects remain recoverably zero;
- true nonzero effects produce recoverable nonzero outcome differences.
